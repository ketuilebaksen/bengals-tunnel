The Bengals brought back Josh Johnson. Again. They released him on Sunday, and two days later, they re-signed him. So the quarterback room now looks like this: Joe Burrow at the top, Joe Flacco behind him on the active roster, Josh Johnson back in the building, and Sean Clifford parked on the practice squad. Four quarterbacks in the ecosystem.

But let's talk about the beautiful absurdity of this room. Joe Flacco is 41 years old. Josh Johnson is 40. The Cincinnati Bengals are entering the 2026 season with two quarterbacks in their forties standing behind a franchise quarterback whose health has been the single biggest storyline of his career. That is one of the wildest QB rooms in football — and somehow, it makes complete sense. Flacco gives you a former Super Bowl MVP's poise and a huge arm that still shows up in flashes. And Johnson gives you... well, Johnson gives you the most experienced journeyman in the history of the sport.

Seriously, his résumé reads like a road trip playlist. Fifth-round pick back in 2008. Fourteen different NFL teams. Stints in three other professional leagues — the old UFL, the AAF, the XFL. Tampa Bay, San Francisco, Cleveland, the Jets, the Colts, the Bills, the Ravens, the Giants, the Texans, the Raiders, Washington, Detroit, Denver — and multiple return trips to half of them. That's not a career path, that's a United States travel itinerary. And this is his THIRD tour in Cincinnati. He was here under Marvin Lewis in 2013 and 2014, came back in 2015, and now — eleven years later — Duke Tobin and Mike Brown have dialed his number again. When an organization keeps calling you back across three different decades of your life, that tells you exactly what kind of professional you are.
